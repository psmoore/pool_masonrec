Aquatics

Mason Recreation  /  Aquatics

Two pools, twenty-six lanes, and everything that happens in them — varsity, club, masters, lane rentals, aqua aerobics, lessons and open lap swim — on one page, in the water each one actually uses.

Competition Pool

Live · today

Fifty metres, two bulkheads, twenty short-course lanes. Use Week for the whole week, or the arrows to move a day at a time.

Every block says which lanes. Tap one for the detail; use the Filter row to show a single group.

This replaces a PDF that is on its eleventh revision. The file is called Comp-Pool-No-Trinity_Master-26-27-11.pdf — revision eleven of a master document for the year. When we first transcribed this pool we were reading revision three. Nothing on the page says which revision you are looking at, or when it changed.

The calendar above carries the whole of revision 11: 65 bookings a week across twenty lanes, including everything the earlier revision had left out — Mako’s morning and evening blocks, the Fairfax Foxes, Centreville Swim Club, the crew swim tests, Shark Tank and Club Swim.

Two things in the published grid do not add up.

⚠️ Friday, 5pm. Lanes 14–16 are labelled “Open Swim 5p–7p”, but the block is only drawn to 6pm — at six, FFX Foxes take those lanes through 7:15. A swimmer who reads the label arrives for an hour that is not there.

⚠️ Tuesday, 8pm. A Splash Night booking holds the entire competition pool from 8 to 10pm, on top of Masters, Club Swim, the Foxes and Nova Meteors, all of whom the grid puts in the water at the same time. One of the two is wrong, and no PDF can tell you which.

Recreation Pool

Live · today

Six lanes and the zero-depth ramp. Mostly open lap swimming — which is exactly why the exceptions matter.

The grey blocks are the mid-day closures. They are the thing a lap swimmer most needs to know and the thing a wall of yellow hides.

The recreation pool closes twice on a Friday and once on a Tuesday — 8–11am and 3–5pm on Friday, 3–5pm on Tuesday. On the printed sheet those are red bands in a field of yellow, on a separate PDF from the competition pool, published on a different cadence: the competition pool is a master for the year, the recreation pool is a single week (Rec-Pool-9.14-9.20.26.pdf).

Two documents, two formats, two revision schedules, one building.

The Masters page and the pool grid agree on the times and disagree on the season. The masters page heads this table “Summer 2026 Practice Schedule” and then lists no-practice dates running from September through December — 25, 26 and 27 September; 3, 23, 24 and 25 October; 7, 19, 20, 21, 26 and 27 November; 3, 4, 5, 6 and 18 December.

A calendar has somewhere to put those. A heading that says “Summer” over a list of December dates does not.

⚠️ The lessons page is advertising a session that ended six months ago. As of today it reads “Registration for group lessons will open for Spring 2026 on January 19th, 2026, and close February 4th, 2026”, and lists class dates in February and March. There is no fall session anywhere on the page, and nothing says there isn’t one.

It also never says which pool. Not in the group lesson descriptions, not in the private lesson descriptions. And the lesson times are on neither pool schedule — no water is reserved for them on either PDF. The Wednesday 6pm class would need lanes that the recreation pool grid gives to Mako.

The Spring 2026 classes are in the calendar at their published dates and times, in the recreation pool, so an operator can see what they occupied. When a fall session is scheduled it lands on the same calendar as everything else — and the pool it lands in is no longer a thing anyone has to guess.

What the two calendars replace

Comp-Pool-No-Trinity_Master-26-27-11.pdf Rec-Pool-9.14-9.20.26.pdf
  • A swimmer wanting a lane at 6pm had to open both PDFs and read two different grid formats, then check the masters page and the lessons page separately.
  • Neither PDF can say today. Both are pictures of a week.
  • The competition pool grid is drawn in one-hour rows, so a practice that ends at 6:15 cannot be drawn honestly — which is how the Friday open-swim label came to promise an hour that is not there.
  • The recreation pool legend lists Mason Life and Streamline as programs, and neither appears anywhere in the week it publishes.
